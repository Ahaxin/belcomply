Will the PPWR authorised representative duty be suspended?

16 Sep 2026 · Regulation

Since 12 August 2026, a non-EU seller must appoint an authorised representative (AR) in every EU country where it sells. Now a proposal in Brussels wants to suspend that duty until 2035. Here's what is actually happening — and why it changes nothing for a Chinese seller.

The proposal

In December 2025, the European Commission proposed COM(2025)982, which would suspend Article 45(3) of the PPWR — the clause that makes an authorised representative mandatory — until 1 January 2035.

The Council already said no

On 24 June 2026, the Council of the EU decided not to proceed with the suspension, opposed by a large majority of member states. The idea is not dead — but it has already been knocked back once.

Next: the ENVI committee vote on 1 October

The European Parliament's ENVI committee votes on the amendments around 1 October 2026. That is the date to watch. But here is the part most coverage misses:

Why it changes nothing for non-EU sellers

Every version of the "suspension" — including the Parliament's narrower approach — applies only to EU-based producers, mostly micro-enterprises. Producers established in third countries, including China, are explicitly excluded from any exemption.

In plain terms: Article 45(3) applies as written today, and for a non-EU seller it will continue to apply no matter how the 1 October vote goes.

The practical takeaway: don't wait for a suspension that will never cover you. If you sell into the EU without a local establishment, you need an authorised representative today — and you'll still need one after the vote.

Sources: the Commission proposal COM(2025)982 and independent PPWR tracking (e.g. PAQR, Tarafex Belgium desk).
