Battery EPR compliance for EU sales

BelComply helps businesses scope battery EPR registration, reporting and authorised representation for their EU markets. Tell us whether batteries are sold on their own or inside equipment and identify the product category. Initial assessment is free, while professional services and third-party charges are separately quoted.

What rules frame this service?

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 covers batteries and waste batteries. The Commission describes five categories: portable, light means of transport, electric vehicle, industrial, and starting, lighting and ignition batteries. An EPR engagement focuses on the agreed producer-responsibility tasks; other battery requirements and their implementation dates need a separate scope check.

What battery data should you collect first?

Prepare the product name, battery chemistry, unit weight and available capacity information. Identify whether it is a standalone battery, a replacement pack or a battery incorporated in equipment. Keep the number of devices and number of batteries separate when a device contains more than one battery. If your supplier provides a technical sheet, include it and explain whether different models use the same battery or different packs.

Why does selling a battery inside a device still need review?

Do not assume an equipment registration settles the battery scope. The equipment and its battery have different product information, and packaging is another stream. Explain whether batteries enter the country already installed, packed alongside the device or supplied later as replacements. The assessment can then identify the information needed for a country-specific quote without promising that one registration or service fee covers every component.

Which tasks belong in a battery EPR proposal?

Ask the proposal to distinguish registration assistance, periodic reporting, representative duties where applicable and external fees. It should also identify the selling entity and each covered country. When you already have a battery registration or scheme relationship, provide the issuing organisation and scope rather than only the number. This helps avoid treating an equipment record, packaging record or unrelated marketplace reference as battery evidence.

What changes when you add another EU market?

Provide a separate destination-country quantity breakdown and explain any change to the importer, warehouse, customer type or selling channel. Belgium, France, Germany, Italy and the Netherlands can be included in the initial compliance discussion. AR availability for Belgium, France, Germany, Italy and the Netherlands must be matched to the actual battery category and written scope. A proposal for one country is not proof of coverage in a second market.

What is free, and what is quoted?

The initial needs assessment is free and does not require you to buy a full service. Its purpose is to identify your products, sales countries and information gaps before a detailed proposal. It is not registration, ongoing reporting or a formal appointment. If you proceed, the written quote will identify deliverables, exclusions, professional fees and external charges.
