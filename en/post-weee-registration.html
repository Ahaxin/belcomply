WEEE registration for non-EU sellers: electronics have their own EPR

12 Oct 2026 · WEEE

Sellers who ship electronics into the EU often finish their packaging EPR and assume they are done. They are not. Electrical and electronic equipment has its own EPR stream — WEEE — with separate registration and a separate authorised representative. Here is how the two fit together.

TL;DR — WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment) is a distinct EPR stream for electronics, separate from packaging EPR. Non-EU electronics sellers need WEEE registration in each country of sale, on top of their packaging registration.

Two EPR streams, not one

Extended producer responsibility is not a single obligation — it is organised by waste stream. Packaging is one stream, governed by the PPWR. Electrical and electronic equipment is another, governed by the WEEE Directive. If you sell a packaged electronic device, you trigger both: packaging EPR for the box it ships in, and WEEE for the device itself.

What WEEE registration covers

  • National registration in each country's WEEE register — separate from the packaging register.
  • A WEEE authorised representative where you are not established — separate from your packaging AR.
  • Collection and recycling fees based on the weight and category of the equipment.
  • Annual reporting of the quantities you placed on the market.

Why sellers miss it

Marketplace verification is more visible for packaging, so sellers complete packaging first and stop there. But WEEE has the same structure and the same enforcement risk — Amazon has enforced WEEE deadlines in Germany since 2023. A missing WEEE number suspends an electronics listing just as fast as a missing packaging number.

How to keep the two straight

Think of it as two parallel tracks that both start when you sell into a country: packaging EPR for the box, WEEE for the device. A single coordinating partner can handle both, but they remain separate registrations, separate identifiers and separate declarations — and both need a locally established representative if you are not established in the country.

The practical takeaway: if your catalogue includes anything with a plug, a battery or a circuit board, WEEE is part of your EU compliance — not an afterthought. Register for both streams, not just the packaging.

Sources: WEEE Directive 2012/19/EU and PPWR (EU) 2025/40.
