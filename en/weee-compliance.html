BELCOMPLY / EU EPR

WEEE compliance support for electrical equipment sellers

BelComply helps sellers define the WEEE registration, reporting and representation work relevant to their electrical and electronic equipment sales. The starting point is the product category, selling entity and destination country. Initial assessment is free; implementation services are quoted separately.

Request a free initial assessment

What rules frame this service?

Directive 2012/19/EU provides the EU framework for waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE). National implementation and producer-registration procedures matter when planning an engagement. WEEE concerns end-of-life equipment responsibilities; it does not replace a separate review of product conformity, restricted substances or product safety.

European Commission overview and legislation · Sources reviewed: 17 November 2026

Which equipment details are useful for a WEEE quote?

Send a product list with model names, intended use, power source and available technical descriptions. Include product weights and explain whether quantities relate to individual units, kits or replacement parts. A photograph can help explain the product, but a marketing image alone may not establish its category. Highlight equipment supplied with batteries, accessories or spare parts, so the assessment does not silently treat every item in a bundle as identical.

How are WEEE registration and ongoing reporting separated?

Initial work starts by checking the selling entity, country and existing records. A proposed registration task should identify the applicant and documents needed. Reporting work should identify the relevant reporting period and product data, including how the customer supplies and reconciles quantities. If another provider already handles part of the work, share the existing scope and registration evidence before requesting a replacement or additional service.

When should authorised representation be reviewed?

Ask for a representation review when the producer is established outside the destination country or the sales route raises a local appointment question. The answer depends on the applicable WEEE rules and business role; a packaging PPWR mandate is not evidence of a WEEE appointment. BelComply can discuss AR service scope for Belgium, France, Germany, Italy and the Netherlands, subject to the category and written appointment.

Can one electronic product involve several EPR streams?

Yes, assess the equipment, any batteries and the packaging separately. For example, an electronic accessory shipped with a battery and a delivery box creates three different sets of information to review. This is a scoping example, not a determination that every seller has identical obligations. Tell us which company places each item on the market, where stock is held and whether a marketplace has requested a particular registration number.

What is free, and what is quoted?

The initial needs assessment is free and does not require you to buy a full service. Its purpose is to identify your products, sales countries and information gaps before a detailed proposal. It is not registration, ongoing reporting or a formal appointment. If you proceed, the written quote will identify deliverables, exclusions, professional fees and external charges.
