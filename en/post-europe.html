Selling across Europe? One EPR partner beats many

30 Aug 2026 · Guide

If you sell into more than one EU market, packaging compliance quickly becomes the most tedious part of your business — a different register, a different producer organisation and a different representative in every country. Here's what a genuinely useful "one-stop" service looks like.

The patchwork problem

There is no single "EU EPR registration". Each member state runs its own regime: Germany has LUCID plus a dual system; France has ADEME and a set of producer responsibility organisations; Belgium has Fost Plus, Valipac and EPRiBEL; the Netherlands has Verpact; Italy has CONAI. Each has its own registration, its own annual declaration, its own deadlines and its own authorised-representative rule.

For a seller active on five Amazon marketplaces, that can mean five different providers, five contracts, five sets of deadlines — and no single party accountable for the whole picture.

What "one-stop" actually means

A good one-stop partner gives you three things: one point of contact, one consolidated quote, and one accountable party you can escalate to when something goes wrong. You stop juggling five relationships and five invoices.

What it should not mean is a provider claiming to run every country single-handedly when it doesn't. That claim falls apart the moment a filing in a country they don't actually cover goes wrong.

Direct vs network — and why honesty matters

The credible model is a mix: direct where it matters, networked where it's smarter. A provider runs its home market itself — where it has the entities, the relationships and the liability — and coordinates the rest through a vetted network of licensed local specialists.

That's how BelComply works. Belgium is our home market: we run it end to end, from Fost Plus registration to the authorisation filed with EPRiBEL, and we carry the liability ourselves. For every other country we coordinate through partners we know and have worked with — and we tell you exactly who handles each country, how long it takes and what it costs before you commit.

The bottom line

When you compare providers, ask two questions: which countries do you cover? and which do you actually run yourself? A partner who answers both clearly is worth more than one who answers only the first.
