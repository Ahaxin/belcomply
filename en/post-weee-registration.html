BELGIUM / WEEE

Belgium WEEE Registration for Overseas Sellers

Packaging registration does not settle WEEE responsibilities for the appliance inside the box. Review the equipment, selling entity and Belgian sales route separately; batteries may add another waste stream.

Foreign distance sellers: check the Belgian mandate

BeWeee’s FAQ identifies a foreign business selling directly to Belgian users through distance selling as the EEE producer or importer, and says it must appoint a Belgian authorised representative. It requires the authorisation to be uploaded. This is WEEE-specific evidence; do not use a PPWR packaging mandate as proof that the equipment obligation has been arranged.

Primary guidance

Distinguish the reporting route from the scheme

BeWeee explains that quantities already reported through the relevant Recupel route should not be reported twice via BeWeee. Recupel describes a collective take-back arrangement through its entry agreement; its guidance also discusses individual compliance. Confirm the applicable route and responsibilities before opening accounts or repeating reports.

Primary guidance

Prepare an appliance-level record

Create a row for each model with its function, brand, dimensions, intended users, battery content and Belgian quantities. Keep technical specifications and invoices with the record. Ask for the equipment category and household/professional classification to be confirmed. A product marketed to businesses should not be classified solely by the buyer’s company status.

Primary guidance

Marketplace evidence is stream-specific

Recupel’s marketplace guidance describes checking seller affiliation or approved individual arrangements for electronics. Read an actual notice by country and stream, then match the seller and equipment categories to the evidence supplied. Keep packaging numbers and any battery evidence in separate fields. GPSR remains a separate product-safety service.
