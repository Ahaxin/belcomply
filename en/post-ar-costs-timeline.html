EU authorised representative: real costs and timelines for 2026

5 Oct 2026 · Costs

The two questions every non-EU seller asks before committing: what does it cost? and how long does it take? Here are real, current numbers — so you can budget and plan instead of guessing.

TL;DR — An EU authorised representative typically costs €80–450 per country per year, and registration takes 2–14 weeks depending on the member state — Belgium is fastest at 4–6 weeks.

What an AR costs, country by country

Authorised representative fees are annual, per country, and reflect one thing above all: how heavy the legal responsibility is in that member state. Belgium, where the AR carries the same obligations as the producer, costs more than Germany, where the AR role is lighter.

These are AR service fees — the cost of the legal role. Eco-fees are separate and pass through at cost, based on the weight and material of your packaging.

What the fee actually buys

An AR fee is not a subscription for "paperwork help". It pays for a legal role:

  • Registration in the national register under the producer's name
  • Annual declarations of packaging volumes, filed on time
  • Authority contact — the AR is the named party the regulator reaches
  • Joint liability — the AR stands liable alongside you, which is why the fee exists at all

The last point is the whole reason the role is not free. In Belgium, the AR carries the same obligations as the producer — that is priced in.

Why Belgium is both fastest and worth it

Belgium registers in 4–6 weeks — up to three times faster than Germany's 12–14 weeks. When a marketplace is enforcing and your listing is at risk, speed matters more than a €50 saving. Belgium's AR also carries the heaviest legal weight, which is why serious providers charge a fair premium for it.

How to budget for multiple countries

If you sell into several EU countries, the fees stack. Three countries at roughly €200–350 each is €600–1,000 a year in AR fees before eco-fees. Two practical moves:

  1. Prioritise by volume and enforcement — Germany, France and the Netherlands are the largest markets and the most likely to enforce; start there.
  2. Bundle through one coordinator — a single partner managing all your ARs under one contract is usually cheaper and always simpler than five separate providers.

The practical takeaway: an AR costs €80–450 per country per year and takes 2–14 weeks to set up. That is the price of keeping your EU listings live — and it is a fraction of what a suspended listing costs in lost sales.

Sources: BelComply's own pricing and partner-network rates, verified against the market in September 2026. Always confirm current figures before budgeting.
